# 05 — Retention Framework

YouTube recommends videos that viewers **click** and are **satisfied by**. In practice, that means:

```
Views ≈ Impressions × CTR
Impressions grow when → CTR holds + Average View Duration (AVD) is strong + viewers keep watching YouTube afterward
```

## Benchmarks (long-form, first 3 months)

| Metric | Weak | Okay | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| CTR (first 7 days) | < 3% | 3–6% | > 6% |
| Average % viewed (8–12 min video) | < 30% | 30–45% | > 45% |
| 30-second retention | < 60% | 60–70% | > 70% |
| Shorts "viewed vs swiped away" | < 60% | 60–75% | > 75% |

Compare to **your own channel average** first; these are rough guides that vary widely by niche.

---

## Stage 1 — Packaging (pre-click)

The **Packaging Triangle**: Title, Thumbnail and the First 5 seconds must tell one story.

- **Idea:** Is this something your viewer *already wants*? (Packaging can't save an idea nobody wants.)
- **Title:** creates a gap ("what happened?", "which one?", "why?") or promises a clear result.
- **Thumbnail:** shows the *emotion or result*, not the process. Use contrast, a face with a clear expression (if on camera), before/after, or one striking object.
- **Test:** Put your thumbnail in a screenshot of YouTube's home feed next to 8 competitors. Does it stand out? Is it clear in 1 second?

## Stage 2 — The Hook (0:00–0:30)

**Confirm → Raise the stakes → Make a promise.** No intros, no "hey guys", no logo stings.

1. **Confirm the click (0–5s):** show/say exactly what the title promised.
2. **Raise the stakes (5–20s):** why it matters, what goes wrong if they don't know it, or the surprising twist.
3. **Promise / open loop (20–30s):** "By the end you'll know X — and the 3rd one is the one nobody talks about."

**Hook formulas**
- *Result first:* "This is a portfolio site that got me 4 interviews. Here's how to build it in a weekend."
- *Contrarian:* "Everyone tells beginners to learn X first. That's why most of them quit."
- *Stakes:* "If you're doing X, you're losing Y every week, and you probably don't know it."
- *Story drop-in:* Start in the middle of the most dramatic moment, then rewind.

## Stage 3 — The Body (keep them watching)

**Script skeleton for 10-minute videos**

| Time | Beat | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Hook (confirm → stakes → promise) | Stop the click-away |
| 0:30 | Context: just enough to understand | Don't front-load; teach as you go |
| 1:00 | Point 1 (solid) | Deliver value fast; build trust |
| 3:00 | **Re-hook** ("but this is where it gets interesting") | Reset attention |
| 3:15 | Point 2 | |
| 5:30 | Point 3 / twist / mid-video payoff | Prevent the midpoint drop |
| 7:30 | **Best point last-ish** (promised in hook) | Pays off the open loop |
| 9:00 | Recap in one sentence + **bridge** to next video | Send them to the next video, not off YouTube |

**Retention techniques**
- **Open loops:** tease something, pay it off later. Have at least 2 open at all times.
- **Pattern interrupts every 20–40s:** a change of camera angle, B-roll, on-screen text, zoom, graphic, sound effect, or location.
- **"But/Therefore" not "And then":** each section should cause the next one.
- **Cut ruthlessly:** remove every pause, every "um", and every sentence that repeats a point.
- **Show, don't tell:** if you say it, show it on screen within 3 seconds.
- **No "subscribe" beg at the start.** Ask after you've delivered value ("If this helped, subscribing is how you'll get part 2").
- **Never end with "that's it for today".** End with the bridge: "Now that you know X, the next problem is Y. I made a whole video on that. Watch it here."

## Stage 4 — Retention graph diagnosis

Open each video's retention graph 48h after publishing and look for:

| Pattern | Means | Fix next time |
|---|---|---|
| Steep drop in first 30s | Hook didn't match packaging, or a slow start | Confirm the click faster; cut the intro |
| Gradual steady slope | Normal. Just reduce the slope. | Tighter edit, more interrupts |
| Sudden cliff mid-video | Boring/confusing section, or viewers thought it was over | Re-hook before transitions; don't say "finally" or "in conclusion" too early |
| Spike (rewatch) | Valuable or confusing moment | Do more of that, or clarify |
| Big drop at the end screen | Normal | Start the end screen with a reason to click, *before* the outro |

## Stage 5 — Editing checklist

- [ ] Cut the first 5–10 seconds you think you need (you probably don't need them)
- [ ] Jump-cut all dead air
- [ ] B-roll/graphic every time you reference something visual
- [ ] Captions or key-word text on screen (lots of viewers watch on mute)
- [ ] Audio: voice clear, music −25 to −30 dB under voice, no clipping. Bad audio loses more viewers than bad video does.
- [ ] Watch the full edit once at 1× speed **as your viewer**. Mark every moment you'd skip and fix it.
