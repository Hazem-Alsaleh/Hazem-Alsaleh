# 10 — Niche Domination: Research, Identity & the First 100 Videos

Module 01 helps you *score* a niche. This module shows how to *research* one properly, choose a position you can own, and turn it into a ranked list of 100 videos.

> The research method (sections A–C) works for any niche. Sections D–G apply it to the example niche from [module 09](09-worked-example.md), **self-taught web dev → first job, documented in public**, which is an assumption based on your GitHub profile. Swap in your own niche and re-score.

---

## A. The 6-lens market research method

Score each lens 1–5 for every candidate niche. Do it with evidence: 2 hours of research per niche, not instinct.

| Lens | How to measure (free methods) | 5 = | 1 = |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Audience demand** | Search the topic on YouTube and look at view counts on videos from channels < 50k subs. Count how many small channels have 100k+ view videos in the last 12 months. | 10+ small channels with breakout videos | Only huge channels get views |
| **Competition** | Look at the top 20 results for 5 core searches. How old are they? How good is the production? Are the same 3 channels everywhere? | Results are old, generic or low effort | Fresh, excellent videos from big channels dominate |
| **Search potential** | Type the topic + a–z into YouTube search. Count autocomplete suggestions that are specific questions. | 50+ specific "how/what/best/vs" questions | Few or vague suggestions |
| **Monetization** | Are there ads on the top videos? Sponsors in descriptions? Affiliate products, courses, SaaS, services? | Many sponsors + affiliate programs + products | Few advertisers, no products |
| **Longevity** | Will the core questions still be asked in 5 years? Is the niche growing (search Google Trends)? | Evergreen + growing | Fad or shrinking |
| **Differentiation** | Can you name a viewer, angle, format or credential the top 10 channels don't serve? | Clear, defensible gap | "I'll just do it better" |

**Decision rule:** total ≥ 22/30 **and** no lens scored 1. Differentiation and Longevity break ties.

## B. Finding the gap: 5 differentiation moves

You don't need a new topic. You need a new **combination**.

1. **Narrow the viewer:** "Python" → "Python for accountants"
2. **Change the stage:** advanced niche → serve beginners (or vice versa)
3. **Change the format:** lectures → build-alongs, talking head → documentary, listicles → experiments
4. **Change the proof:** opinions → tests with real numbers
5. **Change the personality/lens:** neutral → honest, funny, contrarian, journey

Write your gap as: **"Everyone in [niche] does [X]. I'll do [Y] for [specific viewer]."**

## C. Positioning tests before you commit

- [ ] Could you name 30 videos right now without research? (If not: low sustainability)
- [ ] Would your viewer *subscribe* to see the next video, not just watch this one?
- [ ] Is there something you can sell this viewer within 12 months?
- [ ] Does the channel still work if one platform/tool/trend disappears?

## D. Example: market analysis (web dev, learning in public)

| Lens | Score | Evidence to collect / reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Demand | 5 | "Learn to code", "web developer roadmap" and project tutorials keep producing breakout videos for small channels |
| Competition | 3 | Saturated at the general level; much thinner for *honest, job-focused, beginner-to-beginner* content |
| Search | 5 | Huge long-tail: "how to build X with React", "JS project ideas", "how to get a junior dev job" |
| Monetization | 5 | Hosting, courses, dev tools, laptops, coding bootcamps, SaaS sponsors; strong RPM for tech/education |
| Longevity | 4 | Tools change (an advantage: constant new videos), and the core desire (a tech career) is evergreen. The AI job-market anxiety *increases* demand for honest guidance. |
| Differentiation | 4 | Most teachers are seniors; few show the real, current, AI-era beginner path with numbers |
| **Total** | **26/30** ✅ | |

**Gap statement:** *"Everyone in web dev teaches syntax from a senior's point of view. I'll show the honest path from tutorial hell to paid work, building real projects in public, for self-taught beginners in the AI era."*

## E. Channel identity

| Element | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Name** | "Hazem Builds" (or "Build With Hazem"). Personal + action + promise. |
| **Tagline** | *Real projects. No tutorial hell.* |
| **Promise** | Help self-taught developers go from watching tutorials to building real projects and getting paid. |
| **Personality** | Honest, curious, one step ahead, shows failures and numbers |
| **Signature elements** | "Bug of the week" segment; monthly "Honest Update" with real stats; every build-along starts with the finished project |
| **Visual identity** | Dark editor-style background, 1 accent color (e.g. electric green), face + code screenshot thumbnails, bold 2–4 word text |
| **Enemy (what we're against)** | Tutorial hell, gatekeeping, hype ("learn to code in 30 days and earn $100k") |
| **Belief** | You learn to code by building things that matter to someone. |

## F. Target viewer profile

**Primary: "Sam the Stuck Self-Learner"**
- 18–32, studying alone or in a bootcamp, sometimes switching careers
- Has done 2–5 courses, can follow along but freezes on a blank file
- **Pains:** tutorial hell; doesn't know what to build; imposter syndrome; worried AI will remove junior jobs; portfolio gets no replies
- **Desires:** confidence, a portfolio they're proud of, first freelance client or junior role
- **Watches:** coding tutorial channels, dev career channels, productivity channels
- **Watching context:** evenings at the desk (long build-alongs), phone during breaks (Shorts, career videos)
- **Will pay for:** project templates, starter repos, a structured roadmap, portfolio/CV review

**Secondary: "Career-switcher Chris"** (28–40): slower learning, more anxiety about age and time. Serve with "is it too late", "learning with a full-time job" content.

## H. The first 100 video ideas, ranked by growth potential

**Growth Potential Score (GPS)**, out of 25:
`Search demand (1–5) + Browse/curiosity appeal (1–5) + Evergreen life (1–5) + Monetization fit (1–5) + Ease to make well now (1–5)`

How to use the list: work from the top, but keep the **category mix** from section G. Re-rank every month using your own analytics: a topic that over-performs pulls its neighbours up.
